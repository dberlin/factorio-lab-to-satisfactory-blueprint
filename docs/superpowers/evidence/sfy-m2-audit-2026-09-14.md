## What this corpus found, and what fixed it

Two things moved while the gate was being written, and the gate is how both were
seen.

**The first run (`c8e149b2`) was FAIL on one cell.**  `steel-beam-20` in mk3
laid out and the validator rejected it three times:

```
flow.capacity  Build_FoundryMk1_C 2      needs 0.75 items/s of 'iron-ore'  belts bring 0.5833
flow.capacity  Build_FoundryMk1_C 2      needs 0.75 items/s of 'coal'      belts bring 0.5833
flow.capacity  Build_ConstructorMk1_C 19 needs 1 items/s of 'steel-ingot'  belts bring 0.3333
```

The spec is the arithmetic: the foundry group is `count=2 last_clock=7/9` with
`inputs_per_machine={'iron-ore': 3/4, 'coal': 3/4}`, and the constructor group
`count=2 last_clock=1/3` with `{'steel-ingot': 1}`.  `0.75 x 7/9 = 0.5833` and
`1 x 1/3 = 0.3333`, exactly.  The belts feeding the SHARD -- a group's
underclocked last machine -- were sized for the shard's own rate, which is
right; the check compared them against the group's full-clock
`inputs_per_machine`, which is not.  The finding was in the check.

`steel-beam-20` is the first corpus entry to reach it: every flow captured
before this one has whole machine counts at clock 1, so no fixture had a shard
on a belt at all.  Its FactorioLab flow asks for `16/9` foundries and `4/3`
constructors and produces two shards in one build.  Fixed at `729a7e81`.

**Then row splitting landed** (Task 8c, `bdd1e8dc`) and moved fifteen cells at
once: every `rows exceed the designer width` refusal became a depth refusal, and
`concrete-60` in mk2 became a build.  `--strict` named all fifteen as off their
pin while the default gate stayed green, which is exactly the division of labour
the two gates are for -- progress must not read as breakage, and must not go
unnoticed either.  The pins were re-measured at `9c632d33`, and the run above
re-takes the same audit against them at `6a35a02b`.

## What refuses, and what that does and does not mean

31 refusals, all ruled: 28 on depth and 3 on `fluids are M4`.

* **Depth is the real M2 ceiling.**  R10 lays one level of rows inside one
  Blueprint Designer, and `n rows want N cm of band` against 3200 / 4000 / 4800
  is that ruling being applied.  `screw-120` at three rows and 6200 cm is the
  smallest chain no mark holds.  The measured ceiling is **two rows in an mk3**:
  all five CLEAN cells are two-row chains, four of them in the largest designer.
* **No width refusal is left**, and the cause is still ruled because the
  strategy can still raise it.  Before Task 8c there were fifteen, and they were
  never a designer being too small -- they were one unbroken row per recipe
  group, which is a shape M2 had not written.  A reader meeting one again should
  read it that way.
